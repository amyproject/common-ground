# Proposed learning evaluation

This is a plan for a small facilitated pilot, not implemented monitoring or evidence of impact. The UNCCD brief places monitoring implementation outside the project scope.

## Session

Invite 5–8 willing participants in the intended 15–24 age range, with an appropriate youth facilitator. Confirm the hosting organization’s participation arrangements. Use an anonymous session label; avoid collecting names, contact information, precise location, or recordings in the app.

Ask each participant to complete these tasks:

1. Find Farm Park on the cartoon map, enter it, and click a visible object.
2. Change a soil condition and explain what happens to the same rain in both demonstration trays.
3. Save a growing-bed approach, change water or care choices within the resource limits, and compare A and B during the same dry period.
4. Visit deFremery Park, advance a young tree to year five, change its care, and explain the delayed effect. Move the sun and identify why a bench’s shade changes.
5. Compare two shared-space ideas and explain who benefits and what each requires.
6. At the library, use field notes to write a neighborhood idea and a question to ask a steward. Download the journal.
7. Open a real place’s provider information and distinguish the factual listing from the illustrated experiment.
8. Find the Peace Center or Guild view; identify a real event's date/status and official participation link without completing a game.
9. Try the preparation walk, explain one question to ask a steward, and find the next-event or volunteer-interest link.

Observe completion, confusion, and how the participant reasons. A click, download, or stated intention is not evidence of ecological improvement or real-world participation.

## Before / after prompts

Use parallel questions before and after the experience, without teaching the answer in the pre-question:

- How might a newly planted tree differ from the same tree years later?
- Where might the same rain go when it lands on uncovered soil and a maintained, covered bed?
- What could a dry period change in a growing bed with limited water?
- Would a bench near a tree stay shaded all day? What would you check?
- Why could a useful landscape feature deliver less benefit if upkeep is unavailable?
- What does a public-source check tell you, and what would you still ask the project?

Score explanations using a simple 0–2 rubric: no causal explanation, partial explanation, or clear explanation of both the effect and a limitation. Use the same rubric before and after. Do not call the authored numerical game results scientifically validated.

## Reviewer and community check

Ask one prospective Guild reviewer to process a clear correction and a stale or poorly sourced correction. Note the time and the information needed to make the decision. Have a community steward assess a generated proposal for clarity, missing local context, maintenance assumptions, and an appropriate next step.

The [content-review pack](content-review-pack.md) lists the playable routes, assumptions, and pending review fields. A completed pack is preparation for review, not evidence that review occurred.

## Organizer workflow comparison

Ask a willing organizer to prepare the same draft using their current process and the workday kit. Use a fictional date/assignment rather than actual private attendee data. Record task time, revisions needed, missing responsibilities, and whether the result can be handed to another organizer.

Have them edit tasks, role counts, and agenda; download the role CSV and Markdown kit; duplicate for another draft; and reload. Check that the new copy clears the old date, RSVP link, and nicknames while retaining useful edits. Ask them to distinguish a draft role plan from an RSVP list or attendance record.

Review whether the source adaptation and wording feel appropriate to the Guild. Do not claim minutes saved until this comparison happens. Clicking a next-event link does not establish return attendance. A later operational pilot must separately test cross-device use, shared publishing, permissions, and the host's approved follow-up process.

## Success criteria to discuss with partners

- Most participants can navigate from the map into a place and operate its objects and experiments without facilitator intervention.
- Participants can explain delayed tree benefits and a drought/care tradeoff.
- They can compare two choices under the same weather or time conditions, rather than treating the largest score as a complete answer.
- They can distinguish an illustrative score from a measured outcome and a website check from steward confirmation.
- A reviewer can spot a weak source and avoid automatic publication.
- A steward finds the proposal a useful conversation starter.

These are proposed criteria. The pilot is small and cannot establish population-level learning or behavior change. Record a short findings note and revise the scenario accordingly; do not implement telemetry to satisfy this plan.
